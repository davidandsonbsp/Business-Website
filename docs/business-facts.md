⚠️ **Pricing-presentation issue to raise with David:**
- "Regular Cut + Eyebrows = $30" is identical to buying them separately ($25 + $5) —
  it's not actually a deal, so it gives no reason to choose it. Either drop it or make
  it a real value (e.g. $28).
- "Regular Cut + Eyebrows + Beard = $30" is a genuine ~$5 value, but it's the *same
  price* as "Haircut + Beard ($30)" while giving more — so the plain "Haircut + Beard"
  looks worse by comparison. The menu logic is slightly muddled; the site is a chance
  to present pricing cleanly and make the smart bundle obvious.

## Positioning assets (why this matters competitively)
- **"& Sons" = family built into the name.** The family-friendly positioning isn't a
  claim we bolt on — it's the brand itself. Own it.
- **Kids (10 & under) AND Seniors (62 & over) priced explicitly** = a true
  multi-generational, whole-family shop. No local competitor tells this story.
- **David's name already has equity** (see [[competitor-field-1mile]]). "Book with
  David" + the family story is the core message.

## Current visual identity (from the flyer) — honest note
- Palette: navy + red/white barber poles + gold accents; art-deco corners; classic
  serif wordmark "David & Sons"; bold condensed display type.
- It reads **heritage/traditional but cliché-heavy** — two large barber poles, gold,
  scissors, deco flourishes. Competent, but it's the stereotypical barbershop look the
  strategy warns against leaning on.
- **Direction:** keep the equity (the "David & Sons" name, a refined classic/navy
  feel, the family heritage tone). Drop the clichés (double barber poles, deco
  clutter, black+gold). Move to a **modern-heritage** look that's ownable: confident
  type, real photography, generous space, one restrained accent, a custom **D&S**
  monogram instead of a barber pole as the primary mark.
