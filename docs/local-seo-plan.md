# Local SEO & Google Business Profile Plan — David & Sons

> The competitors win on Google + walk-ins, not websites. This is how David shows up
> in the same places and beats them. Prioritized: do the ★ items first.

---

## Why this matters
When someone searches "barber near me" / "barbershop Ditmas Park," Google shows the
**Map pack** (top 3 pins) before any website. Junior's and George's rank there on
review volume and age. David starts behind on reviews — but ahead on everything he
controls: an accurate, complete, active profile + a real website (most rivals lack one).

---

## ★ 1. Google Business Profile (GBP) — the single most important asset
Create/claim at google.com/business. This drives the Map pack and "David & Sons" brand searches.

Fill **completely** (Google rewards completeness):
- **Name:** David & Sons Barbershop (exact, no keyword stuffing).
- **Primary category:** *Barber shop*. Secondary: *Hair salon*.
- **Address:** 1025 Cortelyou Rd, Brooklyn, NY 11218 (must match the website + every
  directory *character-for-character* — this "NAP consistency" is a ranking factor).
- **Phone:** the shop's real number (same everywhere).
- **Hours:** the real hours (and set holiday hours).
- **Website:** the new site URL. **Booking link:** David's theCut link (GBP supports a
  "Book" button — this puts online booking right in the Map pack. Competitors don't have this).
- **Services:** add each with price (Regular Haircut $25, Kids $20, Senior $20, Line Up
  $18, Shape Up $18, Haircut+Beard $30, Hot Towel Shave $15, Eyebrows $5, Designs).
- **Attributes:** "Walk-ins welcome," "Appointments recommended," "Good for kids,"
  "Wheelchair accessible" (if true), "LGBTQ+ friendly" (if applicable).
- **Photos:** 10–15+ real ones — storefront (helps people find the door), interior,
  David cutting, finished cuts, the team. Add a few every week (fresh photos = activity signal).
- **Logo:** use `favicon.svg` (the & mark) exported to PNG; profile/cover from real photos.

## ★ 2. Reviews — close the gap deliberately (the honest way)
David's existing clients are the head start — but **Junior's reviews can't transfer.**
He needs reviews on the *new* GBP.
- Create a short **review link** (GBP gives one) → turn it into a QR code for the mirror,
  the counter, and receipts.
- Ask every happy client, in person, right after the cut: "Mind leaving a quick Google
  review? Search David & Sons." Text them the link.
- Never buy or fake reviews (Google removes them; it can suspend the profile).
- **Reply to every review**, good or bad, briefly and warmly — response rate is a signal
  and shows customer care.
- Goal: 25–30 genuine reviews in the first 8–10 weeks makes the pin competitive locally.

## ★ 3. NAP consistency across the web
Same Name / Address / Phone everywhere. Claim & correct:
- Google Business Profile, **theCut**, **Booksy** (claim the real listing — don't leave
  stubs like Junior's did), Apple Business Connect (Apple Maps), Bing Places, Yelp,
  Foursquare, Facebook/Instagram. Fix any auto-generated wrong listings.

## 4. On-page SEO (already built into the site)
- ✅ Descriptive `<title>` + meta description with neighborhood.
- ✅ `HairSalon` structured data (JSON-LD) with address, geo, hours, services + prices.
- ✅ One clear `<h1>`, sensible headings, fast self-contained page, mobile-first.
- ⛳ **On launch:** real `alt` text on every photo (e.g. "skin fade haircut in Ditmas
  Park"), set the real domain in the canonical/OG/JSON-LD, add a real 1200×630 `og-image.jpg`.

## 5. Target searches (use naturally — never stuff)
Primary: `barber Ditmas Park` · `barbershop Cortelyou Rd` · `barber near me` ·
`men's haircut Ditmas Park` · `kids haircut Ditmas Park` · `fade Flatbush` ·
`beard trim Kensington Brooklyn` · `barber near Cortelyou Rd station`.
These are already reflected in the copy/headings — keep it human.

## 6. Ongoing (light but consistent — beats rivals' dead presences)
- Post to GBP ~weekly (a fresh cut photo, hours change, a slow-day offer).
- Post the same to Instagram; keep the handle, website, and booking link in bio.
- Consider 2–4 genuinely useful pages later *only if worth it* (e.g. "Your child's first
  haircut in Ditmas Park," "How to book with David") — quality over a dead 2018-style blog.
