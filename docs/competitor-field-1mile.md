## The decisive asset: David's own name already carries weight

- **David is named — and praised — in Junior's public reviews** ("skilled barbers
  like David and Luis"). *(Confirm this is our David.)*
- Implication: David is **not** an unknown newcomer. He has personal name
  recognition and, almost certainly, a personal client following built at Junior's.
  Barbers' clients typically follow the barber, not the chair.
- **This is the fastest path to revenue:** make it trivial for his existing clients
  to find him and book him at the new shop. The website's first job is partly a
  *re-connection* tool, not just acquisition.

### Strategy for the David/Junior's dynamic (important, handle with care)
1. **Lead with David's name and reputation — they're his to use.** "Book with David"
   is legitimate, powerful, and honest. His skill and following are personal assets.
2. **Never name, reference, or disparage Junior's on the public site.** Per strategy:
   win by being obviously better, not by looking bitter. Customers will connect the
   dots themselves.
3. **Do not copy** any of Junior's content, branding, photos, or wording.
4. **Flag for the owner (not legal advice):** since David was terminated, confirm
   there's no enforceable non-compete / non-solicitation clause before running an
   overt "I've moved — come find me" campaign to Junior's client list. NY law
   generally disfavors non-competes, but this should be checked by David, not assumed.

## Where David wins (head-to-head)

1. **Booking** — real, modern online booking + "book your barber (David)" +
   next-available. Junior's and the whole radius have nothing that works. Sharpest edge.
2. **Faces + proof** — David front and center with portfolio + real reviews. His
   name already converts; put it to work.
3. **Family-friendly, executed** — the neighborhood has families (Ditmas Park is
   heavily family/stroller); no local shop tells a real kids-experience story.
4. **A genuinely modern, fast, mobile site** — trivially beats auto-gen templates.
